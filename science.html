Chapter 2: Parts of a Plant 🌿 40 Q

Quick Revision:
• A plant has 2 main parts: Root (under soil) and Shoot (above soil)
• Shoot = stem + branches + leaves + buds + flowers + fruits
• Two types of roots: Tap root (one thick main root) and Fibrous root (many thin roots, no main root)
• Thick strong stem = trunk. Weak stem climbing = climber. Weak stem on ground = creeper
• Leaf parts: leaf blade (lamina), main vein, side veins, stomata (tiny pores)
• Green substance in leaf = chlorophyll. Food-making process = photosynthesis
• New plant from a seed = germination

📖 Answer the Following (Long Questions)

1. Name the different parts of a plant.
The parts of a plant are: root, stem, branches, leaves, buds, flowers, fruits and seeds.
2. Differentiate between root and shoot.
Root: Grows UNDER the soil. Absorbs water and minerals.
Shoot: Grows ABOVE the soil. It has the stem, branches, leaves, buds, flowers and fruits.
3. What is germination?
Germination is the process of development of a new plant from a seed. When a seed gets water, air and sunlight in the soil, it grows into a new plant.
4. Why is leaf called the food factory of a plant?
Green leaves have chlorophyll which traps sunlight. Using sunlight, water and carbon dioxide, leaves prepare food for the WHOLE plant. That is why the leaf is called the food factory or kitchen of the plant.
5. State two functions of Root, Stem and Flower.
Root: (1) Fixes the plant to the soil. (2) Absorbs water and minerals.
Stem: (1) Gives support to the plant. (2) Carries water and food to all parts.
Flower: (1) Helps the plant to reproduce. (2) Some flowers store food (broccoli, cauliflower).
6. Difference between tap root and fibrous root.
Tap root: Has one thick main root. Small thin side roots grow from the main root. (Example: turnip, carrot, bean, mustard)
Fibrous root: Has NO main root. Many thin bushy roots grow from the base of the stem. (Example: grass, wheat, onion, rice, palm)
7. What is the role of stomata in a plant?
Stomata are tiny pores on the lower side of the leaf. Air enters the leaf through the stomata. They help the plant to breathe.
8. Describe the process of photosynthesis.
Green leaves have chlorophyll which traps sunlight. Leaves get water from the soil and carbon dioxide from the air. Using sunlight, water and carbon dioxide, they prepare food. Oxygen is given out. This process is called photosynthesis.
9. How are plants useful to us?
1. Plants are our main source of food.
2. Roots hold soil particles and stop soil from washing away.
3. Plants make our surroundings beautiful.
4. Plants reduce pollution — they take in carbon dioxide and give out oxygen.
10. What does a seed require to grow into a plant?
A seed needs soil, water, air and sunlight to grow into a new plant.
HOTS: Leaves of a plant are turning yellow even though you water it. What could be the problem?
The plant is probably not getting enough sunlight. Without sunlight, leaves cannot make food, so they turn yellow and droop. To fix it, move the plant to a sunny place.

Chapter 3: Eating Habits of Animals 🦁 36 Q

Quick Revision:
• Herbivores — eat only plants (cow, goat, deer, monkey, giraffe, elephant)
• Carnivores — eat flesh of other animals (lion, tiger, wolf, eagle, vulture, kite)
• Omnivores — eat both plants and animals (bear, chipmunk, chimpanzee, crow, robin, humans)
• Cud — cows and buffaloes bring food back from stomach to mouth to chew again
• Gnawing — biting and chewing food repeatedly in small pieces (rabbit, rat, squirrel)
• Lapping — drinking liquid with the tongue (dog, cat)
• Food chain — plants are producers, animals are consumers

📖 Answer the Following (Long Questions)

1. Difference between a horse, monkey and rabbit based on eating habits?
All three are herbivores, but they eat differently:
Horse: Has sharp front teeth to bite grass and flat back teeth to chew.
Monkey: Eats fruits like bananas using its hands.
Rabbit: Gnaws — bites and chews food repeatedly in small pieces.
2. What do omnivores eat? Give two examples.
Omnivores eat BOTH plants and other animals. Examples: bear, chipmunk, chimpanzee, crow, robin, and human beings.
3. Two differences between plant-eating and flesh-eating animals.
Plant-eating (herbivores): (1) Eat grass, leaves and fruits. (2) Have sharp FLAT front teeth and wide flat back teeth to chew.
Flesh-eating (carnivores): (1) Eat the flesh of other animals. (2) Have sharp POINTED front teeth and strong jaws to tear flesh.
4. How does a frog catch its prey?
A frog catches insects with its long, sticky tongue.
5. What do you understand by 'chewing the cud'?
Cows and buffaloes swallow their food without chewing. After some time, they bring the food back from the stomach to the mouth to chew it properly. This is called chewing the cud.
6. What is a food chain? Explain with an example.
A food chain shows how animals and plants depend on each other for food. Plants make their own food, so they are called producers. Animals cannot make food, so they are consumers.

Example: Grass → Deer → Lion
(The deer eats grass, and the lion eats the deer.)
7. Difference between lapping and gnawing?
Lapping: Drinking a liquid using the tongue. Example: a cat lapping up milk.
Gnawing: Biting and chewing food repeatedly in small pieces. Example: a rabbit gnawing on a carrot.
8. How are animals, humans and plants inter-dependent?
Animals get food from plants. When animals breathe, they give out carbon dioxide, which plants use to make food. When plants make food, they give out oxygen, which animals use for breathing. Some animals and birds also live on trees.
9. Can any food chain be complete without plants? Why?
No. A food chain always starts with a plant (producer) because plants are the only ones that can make their own food. Without plants, herbivores would have no food, and then carnivores would have nothing to eat either.
10. Give reason: (a) Lions have pointed front teeth (b) Horses have sharp front teeth (c) Mosquito has a long pointed tube (d) Elephant has a trunk
(a) To tear the flesh of other animals.
(b) To bite grass and leaves.
(c) To suck the blood of animals.
(d) To gather food and put it into its mouth, and also to drink water.
11. How should we care for domestic animals?
We should give them proper food, water and shelter. Their shelter should always be clean. They should be given energy-rich food. If they are sick or injured, we should take them to a veterinary doctor.

Chapter 4: Birds 🦅 38 Q

Quick Revision — Types of Beaks:
• Short & hard — sparrow, pigeon, peacock (crush grains and seeds)
• Curved — parrot, parakeet, macaw (crack nuts and hard fruits)
• Strong, sharp & hooked — eagle, vulture, kite (tear flesh)
• Strong & chisel-shaped — woodpecker, hoopoe, humming bird (drill holes)
• Broad & flat — duck, swan (eat worms from muddy water)
• Spoon-shaped — spoonbill (gather tadpoles and insects)

Types of Feet: Perching (crow, sparrow, mynah) • Scratching (hen, rooster, peacock) • Swimming/webbed (duck, goose) • Wading (crane, heron) • Climbing (parrot, woodpecker) • Flesh-eating talons (eagle, vulture)

3 types of feathers: Flight feathers • Down feathers • Body feathers

📖 Answer the Following (Long Questions)

1. Difference between a woven nest and a stitched nest?
Woven nest: Made by the weaverbird. It uses grass, twigs and leaves and pulls them in and out with its beak to weave the nest. It hangs from a branch with a tunnel-shaped opening.
Stitched nest: Made by the tailor bird. It uses its beak like a needle to stitch large leaves together with cotton threads or spider web threads.
2. Why do birds make nests? Explain.
The nest is a shelter for birds. It keeps them safe from bad weather, protects them from their enemies, birds lay their eggs in nests, and they take care of their young ones there until they are ready to fly.
3. How do long and thin legs help a bird in wading?
Water birds like cranes and herons have long, thin legs with spread-out toes. The spread-out toes stop the bird from sinking into the muddy water, so it can walk through water in search of food.
4. What is perching?
Perching means sitting on a branch by gripping it with the feet. Birds like crow, sparrow and mynah have three toes in front and one toe in back, which helps them sit on high branches without falling.
5. What would happen if all the toes of a woodpecker pointed in the same direction?
The woodpecker would not be able to grip the tree trunk properly. It has two toes pointing forward and two pointing backward, which lets it climb trees and hold on while drilling holes. If all toes pointed the same way, it would slip and fall.
6. Name a bird with (a) Chisel-shaped beak (b) Spoon-shaped beak. How do these beaks help?
(a) Woodpecker — its strong chisel-shaped beak helps it drill holes in tree trunks to make its nest and pull out insects.
(b) Spoonbill — its spoon-shaped beak helps it gather tadpoles and insects from water.
7. How does a bird fly?
A bird lifts its body up in the air by flapping its wings. There are two movements:
Downstroke — the wings move downwards and forwards.
Upstroke — the wings move upwards and backwards.
When a bird lands, it spreads out its wings and glides down smoothly.
8. Why is the body of a bird light?
The body of a bird is light because its bones are hollow. But its skeleton is very strong. This helps it to fly easily for a long time.
